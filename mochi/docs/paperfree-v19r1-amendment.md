# Correct the scope of branch smoothness checks

C19 stopped before any optimizer update. Its first teaching sequence completed every tested laptop walking leg, but rejected all three options because a whole-route native-acceleration RMS limit was applied to a short restored history. The W10 laptop branch reported 165.83 rad/s². A complete W10 engineering route reported 160.76 rad/s² and passed the 161.4 rad/s² full-route limit. Segment averages and whole-route averages are different quantities.

The correction is explicit: local branches must pass fall, collision, roll, support and foot-clearance checks. The 161.4 acceleration and 0.00371 torque-change aggregate limits remain mandatory for complete teaching references, development evaluations and fresh tests. No full-route qualification threshold, task verifier, physical controller, scene, action library, step detector or native gait weight changes. Branch diagnostics retain their partial-history scope. All original C19 sources and results remain intact.

If C19 retained a complete qualified teaching reference, reuse it. Otherwise collect one new full reference. That new collection follows a fixed W10 walking, H0 clock, C drawer, P5 laptop, G0 globe and R4 recovery reference on the already opened engineering scenario 185000. It tests the parent's preferred option against the reference, or one alternative when those coincide. The final globe recovery has one measured R4 target. This costs at most 22 physical attempts. Local training targets are the highest step reward among locally valid tested options; the continuous reference executes its fixed schedule. A full valid reference does not establish that combining every local target will succeed. The checkpoint evaluations test that separately.

Measured branch outcomes from C19 are retained with their original sources and observations. Targets are recomputed using the declared local eligibility rule and measured step reward. The audit records the old target, including previously rejected trials, the superseding target and its measured cost. Raw C19 labels and rejection evidence remain unchanged. A newly eligible branch is never relabelled as a completed full route. The accepted full-reference data must supply all six domains. Retain whether it came from C19 or the new collection; a new collection is not required merely to increase usage. Every optimizer update still contains walking, clock, drawer, laptop, globe and recovery batches. Tinker updates Qwen LoRA controller-selection weights. Native gait and book-controller weights remain fixed.

The new campaign carries all completed C19 resource usage, including failed collection and local engineering. It retains the combined ceilings of 112 attempts, 10 simulation CPU-hours, eight optimizer updates, four workers, 600,000 training-token presentations, 900,000 inference tokens, two Exa searches, ten memory writes and twelve recalls. No usage counter is reset to hide the stopped campaign. Reservations that cannot fit must stop execution.

The validated full reference and three parent development records are carried from C19 with their original source and recording hashes. Their physical code, observations, model weights and full-route qualifier are unchanged. They are not presented as new physical attempts. Updates 0, 1, 4 and 8 receive physical development evaluations. Selection uses qualified completion and then fewer steps. Four fresh paired scenarios remain unopened until selection; fixed and static controls use two of those seeds. The full-route target remains four qualified selected attempts, at least two qualified pairs, at least 10% fewer paired steps and no longer paired time. A training-gain claim requires a nonzero selected checkpoint. Negative results remain public.
