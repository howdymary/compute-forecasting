# Peer studies and the Compute Forecast notebook

Reviewed November 14, 2026 Pacific. Exa discovery requested 46 result slots across seven search angles and one temporal-method verification; these are not 36 unique papers read in full. Fifteen primary sources were selected. This is a focused review, not an exhaustive systematic review. Accessible abstracts and unverified reproduction details are labeled below. Current source content is not fed into historical benchmark observations, training, rewards or memory.

## Skeptical verdict

Compute Forecast adds an auditable empirical study of advertised GPU rental offers and bounded experiment selection on one frozen pack. It does not invent price normalization, GPU forecasting, learned acquisition rules, or research-agent loops. The strongest defensible contribution is showing what the apparent policy improvement does and does not depend on: persistence, contract composition, teacher quality, feedback, information budgets and a very small dependent evaluation.

The original v1 negative result remains frozen. New SFT gains on three reused A100 dates are retrospective; feedback-hidden ties limit an adaptation claim. RL updates alone establish execution, not incremental value. Neither polished graphics nor sponsor spending supplies independent market evidence. The registered future outcomes remain pending.

## Important missing modern baseline

[Chronos-2's official model card](https://huggingface.co/amazon/chronos-2) supports multi-step q10/q50/q90 forecasts. It is a relevant untested comparator: our 42-arm registry must not be described as establishing state-of-the-art forecasting competitiveness. The [official report](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.15821) and [repository](https://github.com/amazon-science/chronos-forecasting) establish its identity and October 2025 release; a similarly named later search result was not substituted.

No Chronos run is added to, or retrospectively selected into, the frozen campaign. A defensible next test fixes a model/weights revision, audits pretraining and data vintages, uses past-only inputs unless future covariates were genuinely known, maps the official targets/units and quantiles exactly, and evaluates the same eligible cells and scoring on a newly fixed independent period. Report runtime, uncertainty and failures. Until then there is no measured Chronos comparison and no superiority claim.

The broader [MLS-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.08678) background remains relevant to the boundary between local tuning and generalizable method invention; it is not a matched forecasting comparator.

## Three scientific improvements, fixed before implementation

These additions are descriptive audits of stored results. They do not update models, select a new winner, recalibrate intervals or modify the official export.

1. **Coverage with sharpness and denominators.** Following Gneiting et al. and the distinction in CQR, display observed q10–q90 coverage, covered cells/total cells and mean interval width alongside pinball, by horizon. The nominal central interval is 80%; no population calibration claim or independent-binomial confidence interval follows from three dependent dates. Acceptance: every count agrees with the stored fraction and n_cells; missing metrics stay missing. A poor coverage/width tradeoff remains visible.
2. **Feedback actions versus forecast utility.** Following AIRA and learned-optimization precedents, display changed action sequences, changed selected forecasters and loss changes separately for feedback-hidden conditions. Acceptance: counts come from matched saved traces through the existing analysis, never inferred from tied loss. Missing RL trace analyses are pending, not zero. A changed choice with no gain is reported as such.
3. **An information and execution ledger.** Following MLE-bench's resource discipline and temporal evaluation guidance, distinguish six cached result reveals per task, training optimizer updates and fresh operational Modal calls. Acceptance: each category has its own count and source; equal online reveals are never described as equal wall time, spend or offline training. No new independence is claimed from repeated dates.

All three use public derived artifacts only. Their read/aggregation cost is a small CPU web request; there are no new model calls, optimization trials or sponsor charges beyond ordinary serving/remote validation. Expected request computation is subsecond on these small JSON artifacts, to be checked remotely rather than asserted as a measured benchmark. New training is deferred because a larger tuning run cannot repair the opened-evaluation limitation.

## Three product improvements

1. A coherent paper-and-ink interface: precise SVG plots, subtle ruled paper, a self-hosted licensed handwriting face for annotations, conventional numerals and accessible controls.
2. A movable time ruler and source-bound margin note, plus up to three pinned observations/calculations. Pins capture the original date, values, units and assumptions; changes do not rewrite saved notes. Pins are in browser memory for the session, not a server archive.
3. Research study notes with the three diagnostics above and a linked peer-review matrix. The archived v1 result and running v2 reports remain distinct. Methods and Downloads explain calculations, attribution and publication boundaries.

Every visual note is tied to a forecast artifact or explicit formula. Decorative ink strokes do not perturb axes, points, quantiles or scenario values. The chart is not a probability fan for scenarios. A future usability study is needed before claiming improved comprehension.
